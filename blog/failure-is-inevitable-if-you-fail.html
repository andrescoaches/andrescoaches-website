Failure is inevitable. If you fail, begin again.

October 21, 2026

(above) The Gulf Stream by Winslow Homer

Dearest readers,

Welcome back to another week of reflections on my coaching principles. This week, we’ll look more closely at the principle:

Failure is inevitable. If you fail, begin again.

I love this one because it speaks to the power of a beginner’s mind, and the importance of being accountable for your shortcomings, while making space for the will of the universe, too.

Failures will occur. Period.

A 100% probability of success can never be guaranteed for any endeavor you undertake.

The universe will always introduce confounding factors that may prevent you from getting what you want. These factors are necessarily beyond the scope of your influence, that influence which you exert via your deliberations and actions.

Sometimes failure will occur despite your best efforts.

Despite you making wise, principled decisions.

Despite you taking actions which, to the best of your knowledge, you thought would move you closer to your goal(s).

Failure may also occur because of your own mistakes.

Because you were blind to key information during your decision-making process (knowingly or unknowingly).

Because your wise mind’s clarity was momentarily clouded by strong emotion.

Because you made decisions out of fear, not curiosity, love, nor a desire to grow through healthy challenge and striving.

Not all failure is created equal.

There are at least 2 types of failures. Call them Failure Mode A and B, respectively. They share in common that we may learn from both types if we are willing to reflect upon and understand why they occurred. This requires being honest with ourselves about what constitutes a reason versus an excuse, when it comes to failure’s causes.

Failure Mode A

  • You did everything in your power to make the right call.
  • You thoughtfully considered all relevant information available to you at the time of decision making.
  • Shit hit the fan because of factors beyond your control.

Failure Mode B

  • You overlooked key information needed to make a wise decision. Or worse, you were willingly ignorant of info that would have helped you make a better decision.
  • You tried to avoid work or discomfort.
  • You didn’t do everything in your power to make the right call. So you made the wrong one.
  • Shit hit the fan because of factors entirely within your control.

We hate admitting when we experience Failure Mode B because it’s often accompanied by feelings of shame or inadequacy. As a result, we’re quick to make excuses for our failures instead of owning up to the fact that we could have done better, but chose not to. Admitting our faults takes a healthy dose of self-honesty.

It’s at this junction that I typically see clients move in one of two directions.

For some, the weight of shame bogs them down in defeatist narratives of self-doubt and inadequacy. Their minds become stricken by the fear of not being good enough, and they slip (slowly or quickly) into apathy. They believe inaction will save them from the discomforts of future failure, despite the reality of Failure Mode A always lurking around the corner.

For others, the negative feelings of shame and fear do not result in a downward spiral ending in paralysis. Not to say they don’t feel these emotions, but that they’re able to let them wash over them and pass.

And once they do pass, they return their wise mind’s attention to their agency, which is never lost no matter how many failures one suffers through. These people begin to take action once more and put their time and energy into building compounding habits.

And by doing so they are reinforcing a belief that failing doesn’t make them a failure.

Stagnancy is failure.

Continuing to act is everything. Without action, there is no substance from which to mold our life. Experience is our greatest teacher. And to gather experience, you have to act.

So if you fail, begin again. The only catastrophic failure is not of Mode A nor B, but of Mode C.

Failure Mode C

  • You chose NOT to learn from your failures and their undesirable consequences.
  • You become so discouraged that you stop acting.

Stagnancy is Failure Mode C. Stagnancy guarantees you won’t get what you want.

Of this I’m 100% certain.

TL;DR — Failure is inevitable because you can’t control every factor that goes into a future outcome. Failures may result from our doing or the randomness of the universe. We can learn from both types and should be honest with ourselves if we failed because we didn’t try our best. The worst kind of failure is that which stops you from taking further action. You must keep acting toward your goals, especially after failure. You might want to stop, but don’t. In fact, do the opposite. Start.

With gratitude,
Andrés RM Velarde
