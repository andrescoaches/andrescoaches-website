Your habits matter as much as your goals.

November 12, 2026

(above) A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte by Georges Seurat

Dearest readers,

Welcome back to another reflection on my coaching principles. For fear of sounding like broken record, I’ll keep this one short.

Habits and goals go hand in hand. You need both. One is better than none, but both working together is most effective in getting what you want.

¿Por qué no los dos?

Goals give you direction.

Habits give you traction.

The two work together to get you where you want to go. They necessarily have to.

I used to be a habit maximalist. I had a pretty blatant disregard for the quality of my goals because, after all, I can only control my actions, right? So I didn’t pay goal setting much mind. Instead I focused on building habits that brought me joy, health, and right relationship with others. But in time, my efforts started to feel aimless. I was making progress, but towards what? Did I even like where I was headed? Did I want to go there? Why?

These are critical questions to be asking yourself. I think of clearly defined goals as a north star. A goal helps you navigate and decide which action to take next when you feel lost. It’s the deeper well of purpose you return to when you feel you’ve lost the plot. And, most importantly, goals allow you to objectively measure if you’re succeeding at getting what you want or fucking it all up.

Rest assured, your goals can change in time and should as you sharpen your craft.

By articulating your goals, you breathe life into your desires. You make them real. By extension you make real the fact that you’re accountable for reaching them. This can be a scary prospect because it makes you confront the frightening possibility that you’ll may fail in reaching your goals.

But once you have solid goals, SMART goals, earnest goals — the impact of subsequent action becomes concentrated, more potent. As I’ve said before:

Goals without actions = energy expenditure + stagnancy.

Actions without goals = energy expenditure + getting lost/sidetracked.

BOTH = energy spent on what matters + moving toward your desires.

Most people are sporadic with their habits at best. Or they’re on the other end of the spectrum. They’re too rigid and unwilling to adapt their habits as new life chapters unfold. There’s a sweet spot somewhere in the middle.

Habits define you and compound in time.

I love James Clear’s Atomic Habits because his principles for building habits that stick are simple and effective. It’s one of the books that has most influenced my thinking as a coach and someone who is hell-bent on getting what I want out of this short life. I recommend everyone read this book.

Clear’s writing reminds me that I am a creature of habit. All humans are. It’s our nature. Most profoundly, Clear reminds us that the little decisions matter. He makes a compelling argument that small compounding effects typically outweigh big monumental feats of achievement. Whether it’s a good decision, like having a healthy meal, or a bad one, like smoking a cig, your decisions’ impacts are most heavily felt in the long run. Neither of the above examples will change your life radically in just one day. But over countless days, with enough repetition, these decisions matter a lot.

Habits compound both positively and negatively. It may be just as important to pick up new productive habits as it is to let go of old destructive ones.

Clear is spot on when pointing out that our actions are what define our sense of identity. Our actions tell the truth about who we are and what we really care about. If you want to know someone, notice their actions, not their words. People give a lot of lip service to who they want to be, while their actions tell a very different story.

Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become. ~ James Clear

Fuck around and find out.

I invite you to set goals and habits this month. I offer you four domains of life to consider:

  1. Work, pertaining to your career or other labor you do.
  2. Love, pertaining to your relationship with self and others.
  3. Health, pertaining to your spiritual, emotional, and physical well-being.
  4. Play, pertaining to self expression for the sake of joy and presence.

Take intentional time this week to write down one monthly goal in each of these four domains. Make them SMART goals. Then brainstorm one habit you can do every day to work toward each goal.

That’s it.

It can be the teeny-tiniest action and that’s okay. Start small to make each habit approachable. These will likely be new additions to your daily routine, so it’s important you set yourself up for success by making each habit actually doable.

Remember: small actions compound. So trust the process, be patient, and don’t stop.

Put your goals and habits somewhere you can see them everyday. I encourage you to track your habits physically. Whether it’s putting beans in a jar every time you do a good habit, or marking it down on the calendar in your kitchen — track you habits in a way that works for you and is visible. There are tons of habit tracker apps out there, too.

I also recommend giving yourself rewards for sticking to habits. On the flip side, you may create consequences for failing to keep good habits, or for falling back into bad habits. I dare you to Venmo your best friend a $20 next time you smoke a cig.

Finally, create an accountability system to keep you true to your word. This is where a great coach shines. But it doesn’t have to be a coach. You can rally trusted people in your inner circle to hold you accountable for what you say you’re going to do. This is key. And don’t take it personally when they call you on your excuses. Just do better.

Now get after it! No better time than now. Fuck around and find out your true capabilities.

TL;DR — Repeatable actions, or habits, compound over time. You don’t have to check every item off your to-do list every day. But not doing anything will certainly lead to failure or worse — negatively compounding effects. Realize that compounding takes time. Be patient. Keep going.
