Saying no and letting go are superpowers.

October 27, 2026

(above) Self Potrait with Cropped Hair by Frida Kahlo

Dearest readers,

Welcome back to another week of reflections on my coaching principles. This week, we’ll explore the value of doing less.

Saying no is a practice that highly effective people cultivate, like any other skill, through diligent and thoughtful action. I’ll talk about a few tactics for decisiveness and highlight the importance, once more, of earnest goal setting.

Attention, please.

Our attention is in high demand these days. Everywhere I look, some company is clamoring for mine. As are those people who I’m in relationship with. As are my responsibilities. As is my whining dog, Kobe, who desperately needs lunch and a walk. Don’t even get me started on the infinite scroll that has come to define most of our daily interactions with media. Talk about an attention black hole!

And where attention goes, energy flows. It’s no wonder many of us feel exhausted after a day in the life. Sometimes I find it difficult to choose what things to prioritize. So do many of my clients.

I’ll get into the how, but first the why. Why do we struggle so much with prioritization? Here’s my take:

Because FOMO.

It sounds trite, but I think we frequently operate with an unconscious scarcity mindset. We’re afraid of missing out, so we say yes to everything, or we get overwhelmed and don’t take any actions. (Recall, the latter is worse.)

I think this fun little quirk is innate to the human condition. We’re so painfully aware of our mortality that we grasp for as much as we can in our flash-in-the-pan life. But if everything feels important, nothing is. I think the better way to move through life is to commit to what you care about and grasp for that. Then let that other shit go.

So, where is it that you want your attention, your energy, your life force to flow?

Choose wisely. But choose.

Knowing what you want isn’t the same as knowing what you don’t want.

Years ago, I read Tribe of Mentors by Tim Ferriss and quickly picked up on a pattern among these highly influential people he interviewed. Many claimed that getting better at “saying no” to things (read: opportunities) made them more effective in reaching their goals. And they were only able to do so because they had a firm grasp on those goals. In other words, they knew what they wanted! Do you?

This may be a hot take, but we suck at knowing what we want. It’s much easier to articulate what we don’t want, which is not the same thing.

Knowing what you want is the first step in learning to let go. Your goals (i.e. what you want!) set a benchmark for which actions you deem worthy of your time, energy and attention. Without understanding your goals, you can’t really decide what’s worthwhile or not. A friend of mine is also a coach, and he once posed this question to me:

What are you optimizing for?

This helped me assess whether or not my goals were truly significant. Because it became clear that if they weren’t in support of “that thing” I was optimizing for, they were meaningless.

Are you optimizing for money, time with loved ones, creative expression, physical health, spiritual enlightenment, something else?

It doesn’t matter what you’re optimizing for. What matters is that what you choose to optimize for is motivated by an earnest desire, thoughtfully considered and pursued. Recall, you get to decide.

Learning to let go.

An integral part of deciding is letting go of the other options and accepting the opportunity costs. There are many strategies for sharpening your decisiveness. Dan and Chip Heath write a great book on this very topic. I recommend you read it. Here are some that I use:

  1. Zoom out. Think about the effect a decision will have in the near, medium, and long term.
  2. Avoid making important decisions when I’m in the height of feeling an intense emotion, positive or negative.* Sleep on it.
  3. Interrogate myself on why a choice is in support of my goals. This requires honesty. (I use a 5 Why Root Cause Analysis approach here)
  4. Think in binary. If it’s not a fuck yes, it’s a no.

* I fear this will come across as me telling you to repress your emotions. NO! FEEL THEM! But let them wash over you and pass. I, too, sometimes feel pangs of grief for my paths not taken. I remind myself that it’s okay to feel this. And I remind my self that I suck at making good decisions when I’m feeling fear or other strong emotions.

Less becomes more.

By cultivating a judiciousness, saying no to things that don’t matter to you, you’re making space for things that do matter. Those are the actions and habits that will make you more joyfully fulfilled in the long run. This has proven true in my life. Good luck! And have some fun with it.

With gratitude,
Andrés RM Velarde

TL;DR: Remember you’re finite because you’re mortal. Accept the fact that you can’t do everything and that you’ll have to choose. You may choose wisely. By doing so, you’ll find yourself more energized because you’re doing more of what you want and less of what you don’t.
